About

A printer that grew up with its customers.

Ali Printpack Private Limited was founded in Karachi in the late nineties, printing for the local textile trade. The customers exported, the standards followed them, and the factory had to grow into both.

Scale is only useful if it is consistent.

Today the plant covers 75,000 square feet with more than 150 people. Pre-press, plate-making, offset, screen, thermal and transfer production, die-cutting, finishing, inspection and packing sit inside the same perimeter.

That matters most on repeat programmes. A size ticket reprinted eleven months after the first run has to match the first run — same board, same shade, same die. Running everything in-house is how we keep that promise without a chain of subcontractors between us and the fault.

Energy

Biomass, recovered steam, solar.

Agricultural residue is abundant and usually burned in open fields. We buy it, press it into pellets and feed our boilers with it instead of coal.

Steam for the process side is generated from recovered waste heat rather than fired separately, and a 20 kW rooftop solar array carries part of the daytime load. Together these cut fuel cost by roughly seventy percent against our previous coal-based setup.

We publish measured figures rather than round claims. Emission test reports, pellet sourcing details and energy consumption data are shared with customers on request.

EU regulation

PPWR is changing what a specification has to contain.

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 — the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation — is binding law, not a certificate you can buy. It pushes packaging decisions upstream: material combinations, coatings, adhesives and construction now determine whether a component can be recovered at end of life.

For a printer in Karachi supplying European retail, that means three practical shifts: material evidence travels with the order, recyclable construction is specified at artwork stage, and suppliers who can document their choices are easier to buy from.

Requirements depend on the final format, materials, market and the brand owner's own obligations. We work through them case by case rather than issuing blanket claims.
